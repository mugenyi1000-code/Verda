# Compliance Care Plan — Business Model

## The problem with ESIA/ESMP-only revenue

An ESIA or full ESMP is a one-off engagement: months of fieldwork and
reporting, paid on milestones (mobilization, draft report, NEMA submission,
approval). It's high-value but slow, lumpy cash, and every new project
means a new sales cycle.

Meanwhile, every client who received that ESIA and now holds (or is
renewing) a NEMA certificate has an **ongoing legal obligation** they
usually have no in-house capacity to manage:

- Environmental audits (self-monitoring / compliance audits, typically
  annual or per NEMA's conditions of approval).
- Effluent discharge monitoring against the National Environment (Standards
  for Discharge of Effluent into Water or on Land) Regulations.
- Air emissions monitoring where the facility has stack or fugitive
  emissions (kilns, boilers, quarry crushers, timber treatment).
- Waste handling and storage records under the National Environment (Waste
  Management) Regulations.
- Permit/licence renewal tracking and re-submission before expiry (missed
  renewal = compliance breach, not just an admin lapse).

That obligation doesn't go away after the ESIA is approved — it just has no
owner. EEIS is the obvious owner, because EEIS already:

- Holds the NEMA-certified practitioners the client would otherwise need to
  hire or contract per audit.
- Has the site data, baseline reports, and client relationship from the
  original ESIA/ESMP.
- Can bundle sampling, audit, and permit-tracking work into one visit
  instead of three separate engagements.

## The offer

A **fixed-fee compliance care plan**: a quarterly (or monthly, for
higher-risk sites) site visit plus a short report plus permit-renewal
tracking, invoiced on a recurring schedule rather than milestones.

This is not a discount version of an ESIA. It is a smaller, narrower,
repeatable scope — see `service-scope-checklist.md` — priced to be an easy
yes for a client who already trusts EEIS and already budgets for
compliance as a cost of keeping their NEMA certificate.

## Why the sale is cheap

The single biggest cost in professional services is customer acquisition.
This compliance care plan has none, because the buyer already exists:

- Abubaker Technical
- Cosmic Investments
- Kaletex Ventures
- Lusango Real Uganda
- Crested Crane Timber

Each is a completed or in-progress ESIA/ESMP client. Each is a warm lead for
a compliance care plan, not a cold pitch — the ask is "let us keep you compliant" at
the moment they're already signing something (contract close, final report
handover, or a check-in call).

## Rollout plan (fast)

**Week 1**
- Finalize the three pricing tiers (`pricing-tiers.md`) against actual site
  risk category and travel distance.
- Send the personalized email for each of the five warm leads.

**Weeks 2–4**
- Convert verbal interest to signature using the short-form agreement.
- Book the first quarterly visit for each signed client; log it in the
  tracker (`app/index.html`).
- Invoice at signature / period start, not on delivery.

**Ongoing**
- Every new ESIA/ESMP client gets the compliance care plan pitch built into the
  contract-close conversation as a standard step, not an afterthought.
- Use the tracker to flag any client whose permit renewal or sampling
  window is approaching — that's also the natural moment to upsell a
  second facility or a wider scope.

## Financial logic (illustrative — replace with real EEIS costs)

- A quarterly compliance care plan at a modest fixed fee, sold to even 4–5 existing
  clients, creates a predictable recurring revenue floor that funds
  overhead between ESIA project payments.
- Marginal delivery cost per visit is low: the same certified staff, a
  standard sampling kit, and a templated report — most of the "product" is
  already built from the original ESIA/ESMP work.
- Recurring revenue also de-risks slow ESIA sales cycles: it doesn't
  replace the big project pipeline, it stabilizes the gaps between them.
